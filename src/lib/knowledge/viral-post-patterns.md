# VIRAL POST PATTERNS (distilled from 1,203 top-creator posts)

Extracted from Chris Donnelly (400), Justin Welsh (400), Matt Barker (386) and 16 other high-performing LinkedIn creators. Every example below is verbatim from the dataset. Use these as copyable scaffolds, not rigid rules.

---

## 1. HOOK PATTERNS (the first line does 90% of the work)

The opening line is always a standalone sentence, and almost always followed by a blank line. It creates a curiosity gap, a bold claim, or a tension the reader needs resolved. These are the recurring structures, with real first-lines:

**A. The bold/counterintuitive claim**
- "The simple path to being in the 1% is being willing to look stupid for a decade."
- "100% of successful people are lucky."
- "The smartest career move you can make is staying healthy."
- "Most people screw their life up trying to build a business."

**B. The "you have a problem" callout (accusation hook)**
- "You're optimising for AI, but have no idea if it's working."
- "You can love your job, be excellent at it, and still lose it tomorrow."
- "So many businesses assume they're ready for AI search."

**C. The news/number peg**
- "ChatGPT Ads just launched for 900 million users."
- "Searchable has just raised $14M at an $85M valuation."
- "Google still processes 8.5 billion searches daily."

**D. The credential/contrast flex**
- "In the 2010s, I helped build two $1B companies." (next line: "In the 2020s, I built a $15M one-person business.")
- "I'm 32 and 8 weeks ago I became a girl dad."

**E. The time-anchored story open (Barker's signature)**
- "4 years ago I started posting on LinkedIn. I got mocked every week for it in whatsapp chats."
- "2 weeks ago I started going for coffee + a walk twice a week by myself."
- "Update: my 30 day linkedin post experiment is working stupidly well."

**F. The "how to" / promise + value drop**
- "How to manufacture more luck:" (next line: "Do more and do them better.")
- "You can build an AI agent in 10 minutes." (next line: "But 99% of people will never attempt it:")

**G. The contrarian "don't" / negative command**
- "Don't spend $2,000 on Claude tokens building an automated linkedin content system."
- "Stop wasting months building your first website."

**H. The labeled-mistake / myth-bust**
- "The most common LinkedIn mistake:" (next line: "Thinking followers = revenue.")
- "They say: \"Likes don't pay the bills.\" (next line: "It's much more nuanced than that…")

**I. The question hook**
- "What does \"freedom\" even mean?" (next line: "I think most people have got it wrong.")

**Hook mechanics that recur:** short (often under 12 words), one idea, present tense, second person ("you") or first person ("I"). If a claim is made in line 1, line 3 restates or twists it. Numbers, dollar figures, and timeframes ("10 minutes," "$14M," "8 weeks," "for a decade") appear constantly because they create specificity and stakes.

## 2. POST SHAPES / REUSABLE SKELETONS

**Skeleton 1 — The Listicle Value-Bomb** (Donnelly's workhorse)
Hook → 1-line promise → transition ("Here's exactly how:") → numbered or bulleted list of 5–20 items, each with a bolded label + an `↳` sub-line explaining it → summary takeaway → engagement question → soft product mention → repost CTA.
*Use when:* teaching a framework, tools roundup, "20 concepts you need to know." High saves.

**Skeleton 2 — The Short Essay / Belief Piece** (Welsh's signature)
Hook (bold claim) → concession ("I know that sounds backward") → evidence via short one-line stanzas → the turn ("Meanwhile, the person who kept going…") → distilled lesson → gentle newsletter pitch → reflective closer.
*Use when:* mindset, money, freedom, career philosophy. Drives comments and shares.

**Skeleton 3 — The Micro-Story Update** (Barker's signature)
Time anchor + event ("Last Friday I launched…") → "But…" twist → bulleted result stats → one honest reflection → tiny P.S. CTA with a link.
*Use when:* build-in-public updates, wins, experiments. Feels human, low-effort, high trust. Keep under 100 words.

**Skeleton 4 — The Problem → Agitate → Solution → Proof**
Name a pain the reader feels → agitate ("it's costing them millions") → introduce the fix/framework → show proof/data → CTA.
*Use when:* selling softly or introducing a product/offer.

**Skeleton 5 — The Two-Column Contrast**
Hook framing a split ("Content That Grows vs Content That Sells") → Column A with `→` bullets → Column B with `→` bullets → synthesis ("use both") → TL;DR checklist → question.
*Use when:* comparing two approaches (SEO vs AEO, old way vs new way).

**Skeleton 6 — The Confession / Vulnerable Arc**
Admission ("I never announced it. I was scared to because, honestly: I wasn't sure it was working.") → the low point → what changed → where I am now (bulleted wins) → the lesson → invite.
*Use when:* building relatability and authority simultaneously.

**Skeleton 7 — The Checklist/Self-Audit**
Hook → "Score yourself" framing → categories, each with yes/no diagnostic `↳` questions → scoring bands with what each means → prescriptive next step → CTA.
*Use when:* interactive, save-worthy diagnostic content.

**Skeleton 8 — The System/Routine Prescription**
Hook → the principle → a literal week-by-week or day-by-day plan (Monday: … / Tuesday: …) → the payoff ("In 12 months, your life will look unrecognizable") → the fork ("Or don't run the system") → CTA.
*Use when:* giving an actionable habit or plan.

**Skeleton 9 — The Lead-Magnet Giveaway** (comment-to-get)
Hook → what they'll get, free → "Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send it" → why it matters → bulleted contents with ✅ → bonus/urgency → "Save this post" → "♻️ Repost… And follow [name]."
*Use when:* email-list/lead capture. Engineered for comments.

**Skeleton 10 — The "Nothing's Changed" Ironic List**
Setup ("our life hasn't changed THAT much") → a long bulleted list that obviously contradicts it → deadpan payoff ("Literally nothing's changed.").
*Use when:* humor + relatability. Barker uses this for reach.

## 3. FORMATTING HABITS

- **One idea per line. One sentence per line.** This is the single most consistent trait. Sentences rarely wrap past ~12–15 words before a line break.
- **Aggressive white space.** A blank line between almost every sentence or micro-stanza. The post should look "skimmable" as a tall, thin column on mobile.
- **Line length:** short. Long paragraphs almost never appear. When a thought needs 3 clauses, it's broken into 3 stacked lines.
- **Stanza grouping:** related lines cluster in 2–4 line stanzas, then a blank line, then the next beat.
- **List styling varies by purpose:**
  - Numbered lists `1.` `2.` for sequential frameworks/steps.
  - `-` dashes for quick scannable bullets.
  - `↳` (corner arrow) for a sub-point *explaining* the line above it — Donnelly's trademark.
  - `→` arrows for flows/processes and contrasts.
  - `✅` green checks for benefits/inclusions; `🚫` for problems; `☑️` for TL;DR checklists.
- **Emoji usage:** purposeful, not decorative. Section markers (📣 🧠 💡 📥 📤 📌), status markers (✅ 🚫 ♻️), and one personality emoji at the end. Welsh uses the fewest (often zero); Barker uses casual ones; Donnelly uses structured ones as visual anchors.
- **Bold** is used sparingly for list-item labels, not full sentences.
- **Ideal length ranges:**
  - Story/belief posts (Welsh): ~120–250 words.
  - Micro-updates (Barker): 40–100 words (he literally caps at 100).
  - Value-bomb listicles (Donnelly): 400–700 words, carried by structure and white space.
- **Links:** placed at the end or in a P.S., rarely mid-post (so they don't kill early reach).
- **The `---` divider** sometimes precedes a "how to get the PDF/resource" footer block.

## 4. CTA / CLOSING-QUESTION STYLES

**Engagement questions (drive comments):**
- "What's your favourite set up in Claude right now? Would love to know in the comments!"
- "Where do you fall in the journey above? Let me know in the comments."
- "Are any of these terms new to you? Drop a number in the comments."

**Reflective one-line closers (drive shares/saves):**
- "What is the point of building a business if your life sucks?"
- "Maybe today is the day you start owning your time."
- "Read it and decide which group's table you're sitting at."

**Soft product / newsletter pitches:**
- "Get Saturday's essay: [link]" ("5 minutes. No marketing spam. Read by 180,000+.")
- "Here's one way I can help you write your next linkedin post: [link]."

**Comment-to-unlock (lead gen):**
- "Comment \"OUTBOUND\", and I'll send it straight to you."
- "Comment \"BLUEPRINT\" and I'll personally send it to you."

**The repost sign-off (Donnelly's fixed template):**
- "♻️ Repost to help founders in your network… And follow [Name] for more on [topic]."

**The save prompt:** "Save this post so you can come back to it later!"

## 5. VOICE TRAITS PER TOP CREATOR

**Chris Donnelly** (authority + generosity, structured teacher): dense structured value, labeled lists, `↳` explainers, scoring frameworks, "Here's exactly how." Leads with genuine teaching, pitch last; uses data/authority proof; fixed ♻️ repost-and-follow sign-off.

**Justin Welsh** (calm philosopher, solopreneur essayist): short, aphoristic sentences, heavy white space, minimal emojis/links. Theme is work/money/freedom/health/luck as *choices*; uses the "two groups" fork; identical low-key newsletter sign-off every time (consistency as a brand asset).

**Matt Barker** (fun, casual, build-in-public friend): lowercase, conversational, self-deprecating, British slang. Micro-stories from real life fused with a business point; obsessively transparent with small real numbers; tiny honest P.S. CTAs; caps posts ~100 words.

## 6. DO / DON'T

**DO**
- Open with one bold, specific, standalone line that creates a curiosity gap. Make the reader need line two.
- Write one sentence per line with a blank line between beats. Skim like a tall, thin mobile column.
- Use specific numbers, dollar figures, and timeframes.
- Restate or twist the hook in line 3 to deepen tension before delivering value.
- Give real, usable value (steps, frameworks, diagnostics) before any ask.
- Use `↳`, `→`, `✅`, `-` deliberately to structure lists and sub-points.
- End with EITHER an engagement question OR a reflective one-liner — one clear next action.
- Keep the link/pitch to the end or a P.S. so it doesn't suppress early reach.
- Be personal and specific — real stories, real numbers, real admissions outperform generic advice.
- Vary formats: alternate short belief posts, micro-updates, and big value-bombs.

**DON'T**
- Don't bury the hook or open with throat-clearing ("In today's post I want to talk about…").
- Don't write dense multi-line paragraphs — no wall of text ever appears in top posts.
- Don't stack competing CTAs; one primary ask per post.
- Don't front-load links (they cut reach).
- Don't decorate with random emojis — every emoji marks structure or adds one beat of personality.
- Don't be vague ("grow your brand") when you can be specific.
- Don't pitch before you've given value.
- Don't be corporate or stiff — write like you talk, first/second person.
- Don't make the reader work to find the takeaway — spell out the lesson in a clean, quotable line.
